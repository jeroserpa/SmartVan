## 1. Instrumentation corrections

### 1.1 Register map was wrong — `CORRECTED` in `tools/fbot_probe.py`

Three names inherited from ESP-FBot are misleading; two invert the meaning of
the most useful channels.

| Old name | Reg | Actually is | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `total_w` | 20 | **AC output power only** — i.e. the fridge | `CONFIRMED` |
| `output_w` | 39 | **Total output**, AC + DC + USB | `CONFIRMED` |
| `system_w` | 21 | **Not a power.** Reads 2285 whenever AC input is present, 14–24 otherwise, regardless of 800 W vs 400 W charge rate. Almost certainly **AC input voltage ×0.1** (228.5 V) | `UNVERIFIED` |

Evidence for reg 20: steps exactly with the compressor; unaffected by the
laptop on the 12 V output; reads ~0 during AC pass-through charging (inverter
bypassed); shows the full 2.3 kW induction load during cooking.

**Independently validated 2026-08-19:** Meross MSS315 inline with the fridge
reported 0.352 kWh for the day; reg 20 integrated over the same window gives
~0.358 kWh. **Under 2 % apart.** Two independent instruments, one of which is
not on BLE at all. Reg 20 is trustworthy.

`remaining_min` (reg 59) is junk on this firmware — reads 0 or 42679. Kept in
the CSV, do not use.

### 1.2 Free AC/DC load split

`reg39 − reg20` separates AC-side load from DC/USB load with no extra hardware.
Logged as `dc_usb_load_w`. This is why the fridge signal is clean in both runs:
the logging laptop was on the 12 V output, not AC.

Verified by controlled unplug 2026-08-18 17:41:27 — `total_output_w` 58 → 33
while `ac_output_w` held at 32. **Laptop = 26 W on DC.**

### 1.3 Implied battery capacity — `MEASURED`

Solving the energy balance simultaneously for capacity and overhead across
charge and discharge windows (which constrain capacity from opposite
directions) gives **~3900 Wh usable**, against a 3840 Wh nameplate. The method
validates itself; use 3900 Wh in analyses.

## 2. The fridge: `ESSENTIELB ERT85-55mib6`

### 2.1 Specification

113 L top-format (97 L fridge + 16 L 4★ freezer), **variable-speed inverter
compressor**, 114 kWh/yr (class D), static cold, mechanical thermostat, climate
class N-ST (16–38 °C), 39 dB, **9 h power-failure autonomy**, heat rejected
**through the rear and the top** (not the sides).

114 kWh/yr = 13 W average on the EU test cycle.

### 2.2 Behaviour — `MEASURED` 2026-08-19

Manual temperature log joined against `ac_output_w`:

| Time | Van | Cabinet | AC power |
|---|---|---|---|
| 09:30–11:09 | 24–26 °C | 7–8 °C | 24 W |
| 15:46 | **34 °C** | — | 23 W |
| 16:24 | — | 8 °C | 24 W |
| 18:13 | 32 °C | 7 °C | 34 W |
| 21:00 | 26 °C | — | 33 W |
| **21:44:54** | — | — | **stops → 3–4 W** |
| 13:15 (+1d) | 25 °C | 7 °C | — |

Three things this establishes:

1. **The fridge cycles.** At 21:44:44 the compressor ramped 40 → 28 → 3 W and
   stopped, holding 3–4 W standby. An inverter compressor modulates down and
   *then* stops once load falls below its minimum speed.
2. **Speed tracks accumulated heat with hours of lag.** Peak ambient 15:46,
   peak compressor power ~20:30. Instantaneous ambient is a poor predictor.
3. **It holds 7–8 °C across a 24 → 34 °C ambient swing.** The appliance is
   working correctly. It is not undercharged, restricted or choked.

### 2.3 Withdrawn diagnoses

Recorded so future reasoning does not rediscover them:

- `WITHDRAWN` *"79 % duty means a choked condenser."* There is no cycling to
  compute a duty cycle from at high ambient. Duty cycle cannot diagnose this
  appliance.
- `WITHDRAWN` *"Continuous running with falling power implies undercharge or a
  partial restriction."* That is exactly how a healthy inverter compressor
  behaves as load falls.
- `WITHDRAWN` *"Inverter compressors do not cycle."* Too absolute. They stop
  once the load drops below minimum modulation — observed 21:44:54.
- `WITHDRAWN` *"Skin condenser, check side-wall clearance."* Heat is rejected
  rear and top on this model.

### 2.4 Cabinet temperature is higher than the spec assumes

The cabinet sat at **7–8 °C throughout**, thermostat at its warmest setting.
`CLAUDE.md` §6 assumes a 4 °C setpoint and proposes raising the ceiling to
6–8 °C during sleep mode. **There is no headroom to give away** — 7–8 °C is
already at the top of the safe band for dairy and meat. Any sleep-mode ceiling
must be measured against where the cabinet actually sits, not the spec's
assumed setpoint.

## 3. Energy budget — `MEASURED`

### 3.1 Station overhead ≈ 50 W

Energy balance, `input − loads − battery`, AC load converted at 0.90 and DC at
0.95, capacity 3900 Wh:

| Window (2026-08-19) | Duration | Overhead |
|---|---|---|
| Fridge ON 39 W, no input, 20:45–21:44 | 59 min | 50.6 ±4 W |
| Fridge ON 39 W, no input, 21:00–21:44 | 44 min | 51.6 ±5 W |
| Fridge ON, low input, 18:30–19:45 | 75 min | 52.6 ±3 W |
| Fridge OFF, no input, 21:45–22:10 | 12 min | 30.3 ±19 W *(too short to use)* |

2026-08-18 gave 40–52 W across four independent windows. **Take ~50 W.**

### 3.2 The headline

| | Daily |
|---|---|
| **Fridge** | **~0.35 kWh** |
| **Station overhead** | **~1.20 kWh** |

**The overhead is 3.4× the appliance it exists to power.** The fridge is not
the problem and never was. The original `CLAUDE.md` framing — "the idle equals
the useful load" — understated the imbalance by more than a factor of three.

### 3.3 `BLOCKER` — the 50 W is not yet split

`ac_on` was True for every row of both runs. The 50 W is inverter idle **plus**
the station's own base electronics, and the two cannot be separated from
existing data. Everything downstream depends on the split:

| Assumption | Saving at 50 % inverter duty |
|---|---|
| All 50 W is inverter idle | ~0.52 kWh/day |
| 25 W idle + 25 W base | ~0.22 kWh/day |

**`tools/fbot_probe.py idle-test` closes this.** Run after dark, SOC 30–80 %.
It runs 20 min AC on → 45 min AC off → 20 min AC on, restores AC in a `finally`
with three retries, and prints the subtraction plus the resolution floor for
the phase length actually achieved.

45 min is required, not conservative: SOC resolution is 0.1 % = 3.9 Wh, so at
an expected ~15 W base draw a 10 min phase is under one SOC step.

## 4. Consequences for the arbiter — `CLAUDE.md` §6 needs rewriting

### 4.1 What breaks

- The release condition `output_power < 15W` **can never be satisfied** while
  the compressor modulates. Anything built from the current spec latches
  `fridge_req` true forever.
- The "why 90 s and not 5 min" reasoning about compressor-stop detection is
  moot — there is no crisp stop to detect, only a ramp-down over ~10 s
  preceded by hours of modulation.
- Duty cycle is not a usable diagnostic for this appliance.

### 4.2 Two candidate strategies — decide from the overnight data

**Strategy A — follow the compressor (the original design, salvaged).**
The compressor *does* stop. If it stays off for extended periods overnight, a
simple "inverter off while AC output < 10 W" arbiter recovers the overhead for
free during the coolest hours — which is also when fan noise matters. Requires
no forced cycling and no pulldown penalty.

**Strategy B — impose cycles.**
Force run/rest blocks and let the cabinet coast on the food mass; the 9 h
power-failure rating says the buffer is ample. Blocks of **20–30 min minimum**,
not 5 — inverter compressors dislike frequent restarts.

Estimated, 20 % pulldown penalty, all 50 W assumed to be inverter idle:

| Inverter duty | Daily |
|---|---|
| Continuous (now) | 1.59 kWh |
| 50 % | 1.07 kWh |
| 33 % | 0.87 kWh |

### 4.3 Why forced cycling survives the efficiency objection

An inverter compressor is more efficient for three reasons, and only the first
is lost to imposed cycling:

1. **Warmer evaporator** at low speed — COP improves ~2–3 %/K of evaporating
   temperature. This is the dominant gain and it *is* lost. Roughly independent
   of block length.
2. **No pressure-equalisation losses** on restart. Scales with cycle *count*,
   so long blocks make it small.
3. **No thermostat overshoot.** Minor.

Realistic penalty **15–30 %**. Break-even at 50 % duty and 50 W idle is an
**89 % penalty** — you would need to nearly double the fridge's consumption
before forcing cycles stopped paying. The ratio is lopsided because you are
spending 50 W to run a 27 W (battery-side) appliance.

**But** break-even on inverter idle *alone*, at 50 % duty and a 20 % penalty,
is **~11 W**. If `idle-test` returns idle below that, Strategy B is not worth
it and the answer becomes a 12 V compressor fridge.

### 4.4 Regardless of strategy — the DS18B20 becomes load-bearing

With no crisp electrical signature of cooling state, cabinet temperature is the
arbiter's **only** reliable feedback. The two primary probes move from useful
to required. The optional third (free-air, door detection) stays optional.

## 5. Reopened decision — 12 V compressor fridge

The 230 V unit was retained on the assumption that the only cost was inverter
idle, and that idle was 35 W against a 35 W load. Both figures were wrong: idle
is ~50 W and the load is ~15 W battery-side average.

**Reopened pending `idle-test`, not recommended.** A 24 W inverter fridge is
genuinely efficient hardware. If the idle test shows most of the 50 W is
inverter, the supervisor recovers it and the fridge stays. If most is base
station overhead, neither the supervisor nor a fridge swap helps much, and the
question becomes whether the P310 is the right platform at all.

## 6. Tool state — `tools/fbot_probe.py`

Rewritten. `selftest` passes with no hardware and now also verifies the sanity
limits, CRC detection and terminal formatting.

**Fixed since the 2026-08-18 run:**
- Register map corrected; `dc_usb_load_w` derived and logged.
- Frame validation: CRC where the firmware provides one (auto-detected, both
  byte orders), plausibility bounds, and an SOC slew guard at 0.02 %/s. The
  corrupt tail rows of the first run (SOC dropping 0.4 % in one second as the
  laptop suspended) would now be rejected.
- Rate limiting to one accepted sample per poll interval — killed ~250
  duplicate zero-interval rows.
- Cross-platform keep-awake built in (`SetThreadExecutionState` on Windows,
  `caffeinate`, `systemd-inhibit`). `--no-keep-awake` to disable.
- Reconnect with backoff; outages written as explicit `link_state="gap"` rows
  so analysis excludes them rather than interpolating across them.
- `--hours N` for unattended runs.
- `idle-test` subcommand (§3.3).

**Fixed after the 2026-08-19 run:**
- **Stall watchdog (`--stall`, default 6 missed intervals).** That run lost
  several hours because `BleakClient` reported the link as up while
  notifications had stopped: no exception was raised, reconnect never fired,
  and the poll loop wrote into the void. Five large holes, only three gap
  markers, and the run died at 22:22 with one stray sample at 03:02.

**Known limitation:** the reconnect loop retries on *any* exception, including
the station refusing the link because BrightEMS grabbed it. That presents as
endless polite retrying. The reconnect counter in the closing summary is how
you notice — non-zero at the end of a run means check the gap rows before
trusting the duty figure.
